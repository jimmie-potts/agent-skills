# Trusted resources

Use this reference only when creating or curating `RESOURCES.md` inside the
approved teaching workspace.

Group sources by the role they serve, such as foundational knowledge, practice,
or optional practitioner feedback. For every entry, record:

- title, author or owning organization, and direct citation;
- why the source is trustworthy;
- the topics or claims it supports;
- version or publication date when material; and
- when the learner should use it.

Prefer primary sources, peer-reviewed work, official documentation, original
research, and recognized experts who show their evidence. Label secondary
sources and source disagreement. Add an evidence-gaps section when the mission
needs a claim that no trustworthy source currently supports.

Remove a source that proves inaccurate, shallow, obsolete, or unrelated to the
mission. Do not accumulate bare links. Do not contact, join, post to, or delegate
to any community without a separate user request and authority.
